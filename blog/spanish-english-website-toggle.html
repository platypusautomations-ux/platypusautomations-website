Why We Added a Spanish/English Toggle to a Client Site (and Who Bought It First)

A pill switch in the menu swaps the whole site between languages. One lawn care client bought it first. Here is what it does and who it is for.

This week we shipped a Spanish/English toggle for client websites: a pill switch in the main menu that swaps every piece of text, image caption, and form field between languages without loading a second site. A lawn care client in Oklahoma bought it first, as a $15-a-month add-on to their existing site. If your customers, or the people fielding your calls, aren't all native English speakers, here's what we built and whether it's worth asking about for your own site.

You can try the toggle yourself on our bilingual page. Tap EN or ES and watch the page swap.

The Problem With Running Two Websites

The usual fix for a bilingual customer base is a second, separate Spanish-language site, or a handful of translated PDF flyers that go stale the first time a price or service changes. Both create a maintenance problem: every update has to happen twice, in two places, or one language quietly falls behind the other.

A toggle on one site avoids that. There's one set of pages, one place to update a price or add a service, and the visitor picks the language instead of getting routed to a second, separately maintained version.

What the Toggle Actually Does

  • A pill-style EN | ES switch sits in the navigation on both phone and desktop.
  • It remembers the visitor's choice, so a returning visitor doesn't have to switch back.
  • It swaps the visible text, image alt text, and form placeholders, not just the headline and a couple of buttons.
  • It updates the page's language setting and the browser tab title too, so it reads correctly as a real Spanish page, not an English page with Spanish text pasted in.

The demo on our bilingual page runs the same switch, so you can see the text swap before you decide anything.

Why English Stays the Default in the Code

The underlying page still loads in English first. That's not an oversight: search engines read the page as it first loads, and for most of these businesses that's still the language their search traffic and service-area terms are built around. The toggle means a Spanish-speaking visitor never has to read English to find the switch, they just tap it, but the business doesn't give up its existing search footing to offer that.

Who This Is Actually For

This isn't something every trade business needs. It makes sense when a real share of your customers, or the people who answer your phone and book your jobs, work more comfortably in Spanish than English. It's too new to claim any result from it yet. One client has it live. That's the honest state of it right now: a real add-on, built and sold once, not a feature with a track record behind it yet.

What This Means for Your Own Site

If you're already explaining the same thing twice in two languages on sales calls, or you know a chunk of your customer base would rather read Spanish, this is a $15-a-month add-on to an existing website, not a full rebuild. If that doesn't describe your business, it's genuinely not worth the add-on, and that's fine.

See it working, then ask about it for your site.
