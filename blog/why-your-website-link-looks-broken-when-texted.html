Why Your Website Link Looks Broken When You Text It

A transparent logo is right for your nav bar and wrong for your link preview. The fix is one extra image.

You text a customer your website link. On your phone, the preview looks fine. On theirs, the logo is half-missing, text floating with no background, edges jagged. That is not a one-off glitch. It is a predictable bug, and it is fixable in minutes once you know where it hides.

The Problem: A Logo That Disappears on a Dark Background

We hit this firsthand while building a spec site for a house painting company in Chesapeake. The site itself looked right in every browser. But the link preview that showed up when the site owner texted the link looked broken: the logo's dark navy lettering had gone nearly invisible, sitting on nothing, with rough edges around the letters.

The site was fine. The preview image was the problem.

Why It Happens

Most logos get built as a transparent PNG so they sit cleanly on a website's nav bar, over any background color. That is exactly right for the nav bar.

It is the wrong file for a link preview. When you share a link, texting apps, iMessage, and Facebook Messenger pull a specific image tag from the page (called the Open Graph image, or og:image) and drop it onto a preview card. Most of those apps render that card on a dark background by default.

A transparent logo with dark lettering, dropped onto a dark card, does the obvious thing: the dark letters blend into the dark card and disappear. The jagged edges come from the same transparency being compressed and resized by whatever app is building the preview.

The Fix: Build a Separate Image Just for the Preview

The nav logo and the preview image do not have to be the same file. They should not be.

  • Keep the transparent logo for the website itself (nav bar, footer, anywhere it needs to sit over different backgrounds).
  • Build a second version specifically for the og:image tag: the same logo, centered on a solid white background, sized 1200x630.
  • Point the page's og:image tag at that second file, not the transparent one.

That one swap fixes the preview everywhere it is shared, because the og:image file is what every app actually renders onto the card, regardless of what the rest of the page looks like.

One Gotcha: Check the Preview Before You Send, Not After

Here is the part that catches people even after they know the fix: texting apps cache the preview image at the moment a link is sent, on the sender's phone. If you already texted the link with the broken image, fixing the file on your website does not reach back and repair that already-sent message. The broken preview is locked in on that thread.

The practical move is simple: open the link yourself in a text message or Messenger draft, look at the preview card before you hit send, and only send once it looks right. If it is already out there broken, the fix is to resend the link after the image is corrected, not to assume the old message will update itself.

What This Means If You Are Building or Fixing Your Own Site

If your business has a logo with dark lettering and little or no background, and that logo is also set as your website's social-sharing image, check your own link preview right now by pasting your homepage URL into a new text message to yourself. If the text looks washed out or missing, the fix above is the whole job: one extra opaque image, pointed at the right tag.

A broken preview does not just look sloppy. For a lot of small businesses, a text message with a link is the entire first impression a new customer gets before they ever load the page. That first impression should not break on something this fixable. If you are already building or rebuilding your website, this is one of the small details worth getting right the first time.
